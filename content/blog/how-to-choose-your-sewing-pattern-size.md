<div class="g-quick"><b>In 30 seconds</b><ol><li>Forget your shop size. Pattern sizes work differently.</li><li>Measure your bust, waist and hips with a soft tape.</li><li>Find those numbers on the pattern's size chart.</li><li>For dresses and tops, start from the bust size. Blend to a different size at the waist or hips if you need to.</li></ol></div>

The number one reason a home-sewn dress doesn't fit is picking a size the way you would in a shop. Pattern sizing is based on body measurements, and every brand drafts its own size chart, so the only reliable way to choose is to measure yourself and compare. It takes five minutes and saves hours of unpicking.

## What you need

A soft tape measure, a mirror, light close-fitting clothes (or your usual underwear) and, if possible, a friend to read the tape at the back. Write the numbers down as you go.

<ol class="g-steps"><li><strong>Bust:</strong> around the fullest part of your chest, with the tape level across your back.</li><li><strong>Waist:</strong> around your natural waist, the narrowest part of your torso. Bend to the side; the crease that forms is your waist.</li><li><strong>Hips:</strong> around the fullest part of your bottom, usually 18 to 23 cm (7 to 9 in) below the waist.</li></ol>

<div class="g-tip"><b>Studio tip</b><p>Measure twice, on different days if you can. Most of us vary by a centimetre or two, and it is better to know your range.</p></div>

## Step 2: read the size chart

Every pattern has a body measurement chart. Find your bust, waist and hip numbers in it. Here is an example of how it reads. These numbers are only an illustration, so always use the chart inside your own pattern.

<table class="g-table"><thead><tr><th>Example size</th><th>Bust</th><th>Waist</th><th>Hips</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>M</td><td>92 cm</td><td>74 cm</td><td>100 cm</td></tr><tr><td>L</td><td>98 cm</td><td>80 cm</td><td>106 cm</td></tr><tr><td>XL</td><td>104 cm</td><td>86 cm</td><td>112 cm</td></tr></tbody></table>

If you measure bust 98, waist 84 and hips 110, you are L at the bust, between L and XL at the waist, and nearly XL at the hips. That is completely normal. Very few people fall into one size everywhere.

## Step 3: decide which size to cut

<ul class="g-check"><li><strong>Dresses and tops:</strong> start from your bust size. The shoulders and neckline are the hardest areas to alter, and they are drafted to match the bust.</li><li><strong>Skirts and trousers:</strong> start from your hip size, then check the waist.</li><li><strong>Loose or oversized styles:</strong> there is lots of ease, so you can usually pick the size closest to your bust and stop worrying.</li><li><strong>Fitted styles:</strong> blend between sizes where your measurements change.</li></ul>

## Step 4: blend between sizes

Blending is easier than it sounds. Draw a smooth line from your bust size at the armhole to your waist size at the waist, then to your hip size at the hip.

## Step 5: check the finished measurements

Many patterns also list **finished garment measurements**: the size of the dress itself, not your body. The difference between the two is called <em>ease</em>, the room you need to move and breathe.

- A fitted bodice might have 5 to 8 cm (2 to 3 in) of ease at the bust.
- A relaxed linen dress might have 15 cm (6 in) or more.

Compare the finished measurements with a dress you already own and love. It is the fastest way to know how a new pattern will feel on you.

## Mistakes we see most

<div class="g-mistakes"><div><span>Choosing your usual shop size</span><span>Choose from your body measurements</span></div><div><span>Measuring over a thick jumper</span><span>Measure over light clothing</span></div><div><span>Pulling the tape tight</span><span>Snug, flat and level</span></div><div><span>Cutting one size everywhere when you're between sizes</span><span>Blend at the waist and hips</span></div></div>

## Your sizing checklist

<ul class="g-check"><li>Bust, waist and hip measured and written down</li><li>Matched against this pattern's own size chart</li><li>Starting size chosen: bust for tops and dresses, hips for skirts and trousers</li><li>Blend lines drawn where you need them</li><li>Finished measurements compared with a garment you love</li></ul>
